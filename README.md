# LoadBrief

**Fine-tuning Llama 3 8B to turn athlete-monitoring narratives into structured load-management briefs.**

LoadBrief converts a free-text athlete monitoring summary (training load, heart-rate variability, wellness scores) into a structured, audience-conditioned load-management brief — declaring a risk level, an overreaching classification, specific recommendations, and an escalation trigger, written for an *athlete*, *coach*, or *sports scientist*.

The model, dataset, and a runnable demo are on Hugging Face:

- **Model:** [https://huggingface.co/tyhob/loadbrief](https://huggingface.co/tyhob/loadbrief)
- **Dataset:** [https://huggingface.co/datasets/tyhob/loadbrief-50k](https://huggingface.co/datasets/tyhob/loadbrief-50k)

> Built with Meta Llama 3. Model weights are subject to the [Meta Llama 3 license](https://llama.meta.com/llama3/license/).

## Motivation

Athlete monitoring produces rich time-series data, but turning that data into a clear, actionable interpretation is expert-dependent and inconsistent across audiences. LoadBrief explores whether a small, fine-tunable language model can apply established sports-science decision rules consistently and communicate them in audience-appropriate language — a structured generation task grounded in published criteria rather than open-ended advice.

## Key findings

**Task performance.** Across a held-out test set, the trained model recovers the correct risk and overreaching classification roughly three-quarters of the time (composite reward 0.74 against a reference ceiling of 0.729), with 97% of risk predictions within one class of ground truth and zero catastrophic misclassifications — while the untuned base model scores near zero on the structured fields (reward 0.39, 0% exact risk accuracy).

**SFT saturates the reward; GRPO adds nothing.** The headline result concerns the training recipe itself: **supervised fine-tuning (SFT) alone matches or beats every GRPO variant within noise.** Across eight GRPO configurations (multiple seeds and learning rates) and two reward ablations, GRPO adds nothing measurable on top of SFT — an independent LLM-as-judge evaluation agrees, tying the trained variants within 0.04 on quality dimensions. When the training data is generated by deterministic rules and the RL reward is derived from those same rules, SFT already extracts essentially all the learnable signal. Two configurations sharing a seed but differing 100× in learning rate produced byte-for-byte identical training curves — direct evidence of saturation. This is reported as an honest characterization of when the popular SFT→RL recipe is, and is not, worth its compute.

**Evaluation surfaced two things rule metrics alone could not.** A stratified LLM-judge analysis revealed the model is weakest on scenarios requiring judgment beyond the core rules, and traced that weakness to a data-generation defect (see Limitations). Benchmark evaluation revealed measurable catastrophic forgetting. Both are discussed honestly below.

## How the data is generated

LoadBrief's training data is **synthetic but not LLM-generated.** A purpose-built simulator encodes four bodies of published sports science as quantitative thresholds — Gabbett (ACWR load zones), Plews et al. (HRV suppression), Meeusen et al. (overreaching criteria), and Hooper (wellness norms) — and applies them deterministically.

Generation runs *backward from the label*: each scenario's ground-truth classification is fixed first, then a realistic 28-day time series is sampled to match, and the same rules independently confirm the label holds. Because the data and its label come from the same literature-derived rules, every example label is **correct by construction** with respect to the published criteria. No language model invents clinical content at any point.

The dataset spans 50,000 examples across 66 sports, 18 scenario types, and three complexity tiers (clear-cut, moderate, and conflicting-signal scenarios), each rendered into three audience registers. It is partitioned 80/10/10 into train/validation/test (40,000 / 5,000 / 5,000) with random seed 42.

## Method summary

**Base model:** Llama 3 8B Instruct, fine-tuned with LoRA so the full pipeline fits on a single commodity device.

**Training:** a supervised stage teaches the brief format and the three audience registers, followed by a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) stage that optimizes a five-component reward (correct risk declaration, recommendation specificity, overreaching terminology, escalation trigger, and length). GRPO is used over PPO (no value network, roughly half the memory) and over DPO (the task has a programmatic reward rather than human preference pairs). SFT used a 20,000-example subset of the training split to fit the training device's memory budget; given the observed reward saturation, additional in-distribution data was unlikely to improve task performance.

**Evaluation:** every model is scored on the held-out test set with (1) rule-based metrics — composite reward, exact-match accuracy on the structured labels, and within-one-class accuracy, read against a reference reward ceiling computed from the ground-truth briefs; (2) a stratified LLM-as-judge protocol (Gemini) rating clinical accuracy, actionability, and clarity, broken down by scenario type; and (3) general and clinical-reasoning benchmarks (MedQA, MMLU) run pre- and post-training to measure forgetting, with two similar-size comparison models.

## Limitations

**Rule-grounded, not outcome-validated.** The model learns to apply established sports-science criteria consistently, not to predict real-world injury outcomes. Labels are correct with respect to the published rules, not validated against athlete outcomes; validation against ratings from practicing sports scientists is future work.

**General-capability forgetting.** Fine-tuning measurably degraded performance on general and clinical-reasoning benchmarks (MedQA −6 points; MMLU subjects −4 to −12 points, including the domain-unrelated philosophy subject, which dropped the most). The specialization trades broad competence for task performance, so LoadBrief is a specialist and should not be relied upon for general or clinical question-answering outside its task.

**Override-scenario inconsistency.** A stratified LLM-judge analysis found clinical accuracy is weakest on scenarios where the intended label overrides the raw signals (overtraining syndrome, heat acclimatization, youth growth spurt). This traces to a data-generation limitation: in these scenarios the brief prose was driven by the sampled signals while the label reflected scenario intent, producing internally inconsistent briefs the model learned to reproduce. Regeneration with consistency-checked templates is future work. Notably, this defect was invisible to the rule-based metrics and surfaced only through the LLM judge — evidence for pairing the two evaluation methods.

**Data distribution.** The data is rule-generated, so the model inherits any gaps in the encoded criteria and does not capture the full messiness of real-world monitoring.
